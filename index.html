Why Early Support Pays

Digital support doubles the odds.
Nobody verified it.

9 randomised trials, more than twice the odds of adherence. Every one of them measured it by asking the patient.

OR 2.12
Odds of adherence with app-delivered digital support versus comparator. 9 randomised trials, 988 participants, low heterogeneity. Outcome self-reported.
Armitage, Kassavou & Sutton, BMJ Open 2020;10(1):e032045. Read the study

A patient who stops at month 3 of a 12-month therapy takes 9 months of treatment revenue with them, on top of the acquisition spend already made. The support that keeps them has published evidence behind it. What no sponsor can show today is whether it worked in their own cohort. Play4Health supplies that half: photo-confirmed, timestamped, from day 1 of initiation.

Trial populations covered cardiovascular disease, depression, Parkinson's disease, psoriasis and multimorbidity, at moderate evidence quality. Not a forecast for any specific programme, and not a Play4Health result.

The score behind the metrics

Adherence is not one number.
The HAI scores the behavior behind it.

A percentage tells you doses were missed. The Health Adherence Index tells you who has stopped engaging with the routine, while they are still reachable.

A behavioral signal for programme management. Not a diagnostic tool, and no replacement for clinical judgment.

What the index combines
Dose confirmation regularityConsistency over time, not raw count
01
Side-effect reportingSelf-reported burden between visits
02
Visit readinessPreparedness ahead of each appointment
03
Device return behaviorInhalers and pens returned, verified
04

0 to 100 per patient, refreshed monthly, mapped to 5 stages from Inactive to Internalized. Patients confirm facts, never score themselves.

Where It Applies

Built for treatments
taken every day.

Any regimen where a missed dose stays invisible until the next appointment. The four we start with.

Asthma

Taken daily by patients who feel fine. Silent drop-off, discovered at the exacerbation.

Inhaler return loop

COPD

Multiple inhalers, older patients, complex technique. Hospitalisations track to interrupted therapy.

Inhaler return loop

Diabetes

Injection pens, titration and side-effect burden. Discontinuation surfaces months after control is lost.

Pen return loop

Drug-resistant TB

Long regimens, heavy side effects, resistance as the cost of interruption. Adherence is the outcome.

Adherence only, no device stream

We enter at initiation, because that is where a routine can still be shaped, and we stay for as long as the treatment lasts. Condition-agnostic: all it needs is a near-daily action a patient can confirm, and where a device is involved the return loop runs on the same action. The economics are sharpest on high-cost therapy, where a patient lost early is an immediate loss against an acquisition cost already spent.
