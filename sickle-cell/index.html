The gap

Diagnosis is the start.
Staying on treatment is the hard part.

Screening finds the child. Then the family has to start treatment, give it every day for years, and know when a fever or pain means going to the clinic. Most of that happens at home, out of sight of the programme.

1. ScreenedNewborn or family screening identifies the patient.
2. Reached careNot every diagnosed child is enrolled and started on treatment.
3. Daily treatmentHydroxyurea and prophylaxis every day, for years, often paid out of pocket.
4. Crises at homePain, fever and admissions happen between visits and are rarely recorded.

Questions

Sickle cell programmes
and Play4Health.

What does Play4Health do for sickle cell disease programmes?

It supports families between clinic visits. Each dose of hydroxyurea or daily prophylaxis is confirmed with a photo from the family's own phone, missed doses come with a reason, and crises, fevers and admissions are reported from home. Programme teams see who reached care after diagnosis, who stays on treatment and how patients are doing, in aggregate.

Do families need to download an app?

No. Families use a web link on the phone they already have. It works offline and nothing needs to be installed.

Does Play4Health give medical advice?

No. Families get education written and approved with clinicians, and clear guidance to contact their care team or go to the clinic when danger signs appear. Clinical decisions stay with the care team.

Who pays for the programme?

The sponsor: a pharmaceutical company, a foundation or a public programme. Families never pay to take part.

Where can Play4Health run?

Anywhere families have a mobile phone. Programmes start in East Africa and are designed to extend country by country.
