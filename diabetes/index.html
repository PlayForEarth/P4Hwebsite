The gap

Treatment works on paper.
At home, it quietly stops.

Diabetes and blood pressure feel fine until they do not. Patients pay for the next pack themselves, the routine slips on good days, and the first sign of a stop is often a complication. On long-term therapy, patients take about half of their doses [3].

1. PrescribedThe doctor starts a long-term treatment.
2. First refillCost and doubt make the second pack the first drop-off point.
3. Daily routineA daily pill or pen with no symptoms to remind the patient.
4. Silent stopNobody sees it until the next visit or the next complication.

Questions

Diabetes and hypertension programmes
and Play4Health.

What does Play4Health do for diabetes and hypertension programmes?

It supports patients between clinic visits. Each dose is confirmed from the patient’s own phone, missed doses come with a reason, refills are tracked, and HbA1c and blood pressure results are read against verified doses. Sponsors see who continues and why doses stop, in aggregate.

Can one programme cover several medicines?

Yes. A patient on several medicines counts as one patient.

Do patients need to download an app?

No. Patients use a web link on the phone they already have. It works offline and nothing needs to be installed.

Does Play4Health give medical advice?

No. Patients get education written and approved with clinicians, and clear guidance to contact their care team when symptoms change. Clinical decisions stay with the care team.

Who pays for the programme?

The sponsor: a pharmaceutical company, a foundation or a public programme. Patients never pay to take part.
