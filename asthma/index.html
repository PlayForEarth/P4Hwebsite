The gap

The preventer works best
when nothing seems to happen.

When breathing is fine, the preventer feels useless and stops. The reliever takes over, attacks return, and the cycle repeats out of sight. On long-term therapy, patients take about half of their doses [2].

1. PrescribedA preventer inhaler is started, often with a reliever.
2. Good weeksSymptoms ease, and the daily preventer starts to feel optional.
3. Reliever onlyThe reliever replaces the preventer. Inflammation stays untreated.
4. Attacks at homeNight symptoms and attacks happen between visits, rarely recorded.

Questions

Asthma programmes
and Play4Health.

What does Play4Health do for asthma programmes?

It supports patients and parents between clinic visits. Each preventer dose is confirmed from the family’s own phone, missed doses come with a reason, and asthma control and attacks are reported from home. Sponsors see preventer continuity, reliever use and side effects from the same patients, in aggregate.

Can patients return empty inhalers?

Yes, as an option. Empty inhalers are returned at the pharmacy at refill, photo-verified and linked to the same patient record as the doses.

Do patients need to download an app?

No. Patients use a web link on the phone they already have. It works offline and nothing needs to be installed.

Does Play4Health give medical advice?

No. Patients get education written and approved with clinicians, and clear guidance to contact their care team when symptoms change. Clinical decisions stay with the care team.

Who pays for the programme?

The sponsor: a pharmaceutical company, a foundation or a public programme. Patients never pay to take part.
